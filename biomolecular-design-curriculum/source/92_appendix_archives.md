# Part VI — Appendices

## Appendix A — The Standing Archives, Enumerated

The Atlas selects. This appendix does not: it is the complete talk-level index
of the archives that matter, so that when you want something the Atlas did not
choose, you can find it without re-doing the search.

**Why enumerate instead of linking the channel.** A channel link is an
invitation to scroll. An enumerated archive is a thing you can grep, sort by
runtime, and schedule. It also makes the gaps visible — and the gaps turned out
to be informative, which is the subject of A.9.

Every entry below came from the live listing (`yt-dlp --flat-playlist` against
the channel or playlist), joined against each series' official archive page for
speaker and affiliation. Nothing here is reconstructed from memory.

### A.9 What the gaps tell you

Enumerating these archives surfaced absences that are more informative than any
individual entry. Four are worth stating plainly.

**1. The pre-2021 seminar record is simply gone.** BPDMC's 2018 and 2019
archives list 28 talks with speakers, titles and dates, and not one was
recorded. Seven more from 2020 and two from 2021 are likewise text-only. The
field's institutional memory from before the pandemic normalized recording is
a list of titles. If you ever run a seminar, record it.

**2. Some of the most-cited people give the fewest talks.** Tanja Kortemme,
one of the ten scientists you named, has almost no recorded talks anywhere;
her ideas reach you through papers and through students like Anum Glasgow
(A.2 #58). Phil Bradley has no first-author talk on TCRdock despite TCR-pMHC
modeling being a field of its own. **Visibility on video and influence in the
literature are weakly correlated, and treating a talk archive as a map of the
field would systematically mislead you.** The Atlas corrects for this by
pairing papers to talks rather than organizing around talks alone.

**3. Nine design methods in Atlas R have no talk at all** — ProteinSolver,
PiFold, ProstT5, ProteinSGM, BoltzGen (until a 2026 workflow session),
PocketGen, AlphaProteo, Kortemme's biosensors, and the classical
theozyme/RosettaMatch line. Several are from industry labs (DeepMind's
AlphaProteo) and several are from groups that publish without presenting. The
pattern is real: **the seminar circuit over-represents academic groups with
strong US presences and under-represents industry and non-US work.**

**4. The negative results are the least-recorded of all.** Blomberg et al.
2013 — which showed the famous Kemp eliminase's catalysis came from directed
evolution rather than design — has no talk. Neither does the Kortemme biosensor
work. Meanwhile every advocacy result has three recordings across three venues.
**This asymmetry is a property of how science is communicated, not of what is
true**, and compensating for it is the single most useful habit this curriculum
can give you. When a method has three enthusiastic talks and no critical one,
that is not evidence the method is uncontested. It is evidence that criticism
does not get invited.
