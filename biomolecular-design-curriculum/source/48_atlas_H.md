# Atlas H — Polymer Theory and Minimal Models

> **Problem.** What does a chain do, independent of its chemistry? A protein is a
> heteropolymer, DNA is a semiflexible polymer, chromatin is a polymer of
> polymers. Before any of the specific interactions matter, the fact of being a
> connected chain already determines a great deal — the distribution of
> end-to-end distances, the entropic cost of stretching, the scaling of size with
> length, the dynamics of relaxation. **Most of the intuition people call
> "biophysical" is really polymer physics they absorbed without deriving.**

This family is the shortest route to being able to estimate things. It is also
where the intrinsically disordered proteome lives, which is most of the proteome.

## H.6 BUILD — Atlas H

1. **Simulate an ideal chain and a self-avoiding walk.** Measure the scaling
   exponent for each. Recover ν = 1/2 and ν ≈ 0.588. Then do it in two
   dimensions and explain the difference.
2. **Derive and then numerically verify the worm-like chain force-extension
   relation.** Fit a persistence length to published DNA stretching data.
3. **Implement the HP lattice model** on a 2D square lattice. Enumerate all
   conformations for a 16-mer. Count how many sequences have a unique ground
   state. That number is designability, and it is checkpoint 23 made concrete.
4. **The disorder exercise.** Take an IDP sequence you care about. Predict
   its radius of gyration from polymer scaling using sequence composition.
   Compare to any available SAXS or FRET measurement. Then ask what AlphaFold
   predicted and what that prediction could possibly have meant.

**Derivation checkpoints due: 31, 32, 33.**
