# Part I — Foundations

## B.0 What this part is for

The distinction this part creates is between someone who can *apply* methods and
someone who can *derive* them. Those look similar on a CV and are entirely
different in practice. The applier reads a new paper and learns what the method
does. The deriver reads the same paper, sees which lemma is doing the work,
notices it holds under weaker assumptions than the authors used, and has a new
method by Friday.

Everything here is enumerated lecture by lecture with runtimes, so each study
session has a specific named target. The counts are real — they were read from
live playlists at compile time — and where a course has gaps or mislabeled
items, that is noted inline rather than left for you to discover at 11pm.

Six modules:

| | Module | What it gives you |
|---|---|---|
| **B.1** | Mathematics | Linear algebra, matrix calculus, probability, stochastic processes, information theory |
| **B.2** | Statistical mechanics | Equilibrium and nonequilibrium, Langevin, Fokker-Planck, linear response |
| **B.3** | Computer science | Algorithms, combinatorial optimization, graphical models, parallel computing, ML systems |
| **B.4** | Geometry and symmetry | Manifolds, Lie groups, representation theory, equivariant architectures |
| **B.5** | Chemistry and structural biology | What the molecules actually are, and what a structure actually is |
| **B.6** | Deep learning foundations | Backpropagation through transformers, generative modeling, built from scratch |

Order matters less than depth. The one hard dependency: **B.4 before any
equivariant architecture work**, and **B.2 before Atlas families D, E and F.**
