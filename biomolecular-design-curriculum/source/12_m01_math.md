**Why lectures 7, 13 and 17 matter to you specifically.** Eckart-Young is the
theorem that says the best low-rank approximation is the truncated SVD —
which is the mathematical justification for every attempt to compress the pair
representation in a structure predictor. Lecture 17 on rapidly decreasing
singular values tells you *when* that compression will work. Given that your
AF3-chromatin work is memory-bound on the triangle operations, this is not
abstract: it is the theory of whether the thing you want to do is possible.

Lecture 34, on distance matrices and the Procrustes problem, is the direct
mathematical ancestor of structural superposition and of the Kabsch algorithm
you use every time you compute an RMSD.

### MIT 18.S096 — Matrix Calculus for Machine Learning and Beyond
**Steven Johnson & Alan Edelman (MIT)** · 17 videos · ~14 hours · Tier A

**The highest-leverage item in this module and the one almost nobody takes.**
Matrix calculus is what lets you derive adjoint and reverse-mode gradients
through a simulation or an implicit solver — the skill needed to differentiate
through molecular dynamics, a Poisson-Boltzmann solve, or a docking inner loop.
Differentiable simulation is one of the cleanest open directions in this field
and essentially nobody entering it from biology has the calculus to attempt it.

Lecture 6.1 is the single most important video in this module. The adjoint
method for ODEs is how you differentiate through a trajectory without storing

simulation.

Lecture 8.1 on derivatives of eigenproblems is the one to watch if you ever want
to optimize a normal-mode spectrum — which is Atlas family I's most obvious
unexploited design objective.

## B.1.3 Information theory

### David MacKay — Information Theory, Pattern Recognition and Neural Networks
**University of Cambridge** · 16 lectures · ~18 hours · Tier A

One of the great lecture series in any subject. MacKay died in 2016; the
lectures remain, and they are directly relevant in a way that is easy to miss.
Entropy, typicality and the minimum-description-length view give you the
language to argue that a sequence-design model has learned real biophysical
constraint rather than dataset bias — which is the central unsettled question of
Atlas family Q.

## B.1.4 BUILD — Module B.1

1. **Implement PCA and TICA from scratch** on one of your own trajectories.
   Articulate why TICA finds slow coordinates where PCA finds high-variance ones.
2. **Implement Metropolis-Hastings** for a nontrivial target and *prove* — on
   paper, then verify numerically — that your proposal and acceptance rule
   satisfy detailed balance.
3. **Reverse-mode differentiation through a simulation.** Differentiate a
   three-step gradient descent on a toy energy function with respect to the
   energy parameters, by hand and then with autograd. Then do it with the adjoint
   method and compare memory usage. This is 18.S096 lecture 6.1 made concrete and
   it is the seed of differentiable simulation.
4. **Compute per-position entropy of a protein family alignment.** Compare to
   burial from a structure. What is the mutual information between conservation
   and burial, and what does the residual tell you?

**Derivation checkpoints due: 56 (backpropagation and the adjoint method).**
